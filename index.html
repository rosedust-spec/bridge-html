The problem

Language is the last barrier nobody solved at work.

Multinational teams default to English — not because everyone is comfortable in it, but because it is the only common ground. That is a tax. Every meeting, message, and negotiation costs more when half the room is operating in their second or third language.

A recruiter who speaks a candidate's language doesn't just communicate better — they close more. A company where every teammate speaks multiple languages doesn't just collaborate better — they think differently.

Bridge doesn't ask anyone to study. It makes vocabulary retention, phrase familiarity, and cultural understanding happen as a byproduct of conversations they are already having.

What it is

Lives inside your chat. Follows you when you leave.

Bridge has two states — and it moves between them automatically, the same way Facebook Messenger does.

①

Embedded — when you're in the chat

When you're on LinkedIn, Slack, WhatsApp Web, or Gmail, Bridge lives inside the chat interface itself. Bilingual translation appears inline below each message. The compose preview is part of the input area. It feels native — not bolted on top. No overlay, no separate panel. Just the chat, with language built in.

②

Floating bubble — when you leave

The moment you navigate to a different tab, the active conversation detaches and becomes a floating chat head — exactly like Messenger. It follows you across every tab. Click to expand a mini chat window with the full bilingual view and compose panel. Reply without going back to the platform. Close or minimise any time.

The result: a conversation you started on LinkedIn can be replied to from a Google Doc tab, a Notion page, or your ATS — without ever switching back. The language layer travels with it.

"Learning a language should be as accessible as trying food from another country."

Core product philosophy

The three axes that make it different from every existing tool:

①

Comfort language — any language

You set whatever language you think in best — Urdu, Tagalog, Yoruba, Arabic, English. All translations, notes, and suggestions are delivered in that language. Not English by default. No assumptions.

②

Target language — what you're learning

The language of the person you are speaking with, or the language you are deliberately trying to acquire through real work conversations.

③

Render mode — how it appears to you

Native script, romanized transliteration, or smart mix. You progress naturally from Roman script toward native script as comfort builds — without manually changing settings.

✦

Smart mix — the differentiator

Cultural phrases ("insha allah", "ore mi", "salamat") are never sanitized. Familiar phrases stay in your language as anchors. Only genuinely new vocabulary is surfaced in the target language.

⟳

How language is actually learned here

Every phrase you encounter in a real conversation is tracked automatically. The first time you see "Báwo ni" it is fully translated. By the third encounter, the translation appears dimmer. By the seventh, you tap to confirm you know it. By the fifteenth, no hint appears at all — the phrase is yours. No flashcard ever created. No drill ever run. This is how children learn language: through repeated exposure in context, not repetition out of context.

⊕

Multinational teams: lower cognitive load, higher output

Employees forced to operate entirely in a second language carry a measurable cognitive tax on every message, meeting, and decision. Bridge lets each team member read and write in their strongest language while still communicating across language lines — reducing that tax without requiring anyone to change how they work.

◈

Register and cultural fluency, not just word meaning

Every phrase is tagged with tone (formal vs. casual) and cultural context. "Insha allah" is not just "God willing" — in some contexts it signals genuine hope, in others polite deflection. Bridge explains this in your comfort language, at the moment it appears in a real conversation. That is how sociolinguistic intuition is built: from live context, not a grammar book.

⊞

A team that becomes multilingual through work, not training

After six months of daily use on a team plan, each member will have naturally retained hundreds of phrases from real conversations — greetings, professional phrases, culturally specific expressions. They didn't take a class. They just did their jobs. That compound effect across a whole company is a strategic differentiator no competitor can replicate through a hiring policy alone.

Recruitment impact

How Bridge improves hiring internationally.

Most international hiring fails not at the screening stage but at the human stage — rapport, tone, trust. Language is the vehicle for all three.

Stage 1

First contact

Recruiter reaches out in their own language as usual. Optionally, Bridge generates a preview of the message in the candidate's language — the recruiter chooses whether to send it translated or not.

Stage 2

Live conversation

Every reply appears in the recruiter's comfort language. New phrases are highlighted and explained in context — not as a list to memorise, but as they naturally surface. Repeated phrases begin to lose their translation hints. The recruiter starts recognising words without looking.

Stage 3

Qualification

Recruiter asks questions in target language with tone guidance — formal or casual adjusted to match the candidate's register. Notes saved per candidate in their language.

Stage 4

Offer & close

After 4–6 weeks of active conversations, the recruiter has naturally retained greetings, availability phrases, and professional expressions in the candidate's language — without a single study session. The relationship, and the language, closes the deal.

A recruiter who speaks the language of a candidate is not just a better communicator — they are a signal that the company respects where that candidate comes from.

UI / UX overview

Two states. One experience.

Bridge behaves differently depending on where you are — embedded when you're in the chat, a floating bubble when you leave. The language layer never drops, regardless of which state you're in.

Think Messenger chat heads — but for every professional platform, with a bilingual layer built into every conversation.

State 1 — Embedded (you're on the platform)

  • Bridge injects directly into LinkedIn DMs, Slack threads, WhatsApp Web, and Gmail — no overlay, no separate panel
  • Every incoming message: original text on top, comfort language translation inline below it
  • Phrase notes appear beneath the translation — every phrase colour-coded and broken down
  • Compose area: your input on the left, live target language preview on the right — built into the platform's own text box
  • Tone toggle (Casual / Formal) sits above the compose area — one click, preview updates instantly
  • Feels like the platform built it themselves

State 2 — Floating bubble (you've left the platform)

  • The moment you navigate away from the chat platform, the active conversation detaches into a floating chat head
  • Small avatar bubble — draggable, sits in the corner of any tab, unobtrusive
  • Click to expand: full mini chat window with bilingual view, phrase notes, and compose panel — all in the bubble
  • Reply directly from the bubble without going back to LinkedIn, Slack, or WhatsApp
  • Multiple conversations can each have their own bubble — like Messenger chat heads
  • Minimise any bubble individually or dismiss when the conversation is done

Bilingual chat view (both states)

  • Every phrase in every message broken down individually
  • Colour-coded: purple (new) · green (yours) · amber (cultural) · grey (fading)
  • Always visible — no clicking to reveal translations
  • Cultural phrases preserved as-is with a one-time context note

Compose panel (both states)

  • Type in your comfort language — target language preview appears live alongside
  • Tone toggle: Casual / Formal — updates in real time
  • "More natural" AI suggestions below the preview
  • Context note warns if your tone mismatches the conversation's register

Phrase memory — 4 exposure stages

  • Seen 1×: full translation always visible
  • Seen 3×: translation shown dimmer — system starts trusting you
  • Seen 7×: tap to reveal — almost yours
  • Seen 15×: no hint — the phrase is retained

Language features (both states)

  • Tone & register tags — formal vs. casual on every phrase
  • Cultural context tooltip — tap any phrase for meaning, usage, cultural signal
  • Script bridge — Roman → mixed → native script, automatic progression
  • Conversation phrase bank — save any phrase with one tap

Ambient progress

  • No streaks, no points, no leaderboards
  • Monthly: "You retained 47 Yoruba phrases from real conversations"
  • Lists the actual phrases — and where you first saw each one
  • Tracks phrase retention, register familiarity, script progress
Technical stack

Lightweight. Defensible. Buildable.

Extension framework

Plasmo Framework (MV3, React, Tailwind) — handles both content script injection for embedded mode and the persistent background service worker + floating iframe for the bubble state. Built specifically for this kind of dual-mode browser behaviour.

Backend

Supabase — auth, contact notes, phrase memory, team data. Edge Functions for translation orchestration. Clean and fast to ship MVP on.

Translation engine

OpenAI API — tone preservation, smart mix, cultural phrase detection, compose suggestions. Not Google Translate. That's the technical moat.

Legal posture

Manual-triggered, user-controlled. An AI communication assistant — not an automation bot. Never initiates messages, never background-monitors, never scrapes.
